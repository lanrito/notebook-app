What it is

The Director’s Notebook keeps a scene’s breakdown, strategy, floor plan and shot list in one place. You work on one scene at a time, and the four tabs at the top are four views of that same scene.

It runs in a browser and can be installed as an app. Once it has been opened on a device it keeps working without a connection.

Installing it

Where: app.thedirectorsnotebook.com

On the iPad

  1. Open the address in Safari.
  2. Tap Share, then Add to Home Screen.
  3. Open it from the Home Screen from then on.

On a Mac or a PC

  1. Open the address in Chrome.
  2. Use Chrome’s menu to install the page as an app, then keep it in the Dock or on the taskbar.

On an Android tablet

  1. Open the address in Chrome.
  2. Open Chrome’s menu and choose Add to Home screen.
  3. Open it from the Home screen from then on.

On a phone

  1. Open the address in Safari on an iPhone, or Chrome on an Android phone.
  2. Add it to the Home Screen the same way as on a tablet.

On a phone the app shows one thing at a time, and a row of buttons at the bottom switches between them. Everything in this guide is there; the pictures show the wider screen.

The app updates itself. When a new version is ready it reloads at a quiet moment, or shows a Reload bar if you are in the middle of something. The version date is in Menu › Sync & Backup.

Your first five minutes

The shortest way to see what the notebook does. It takes one empty scene.

  1. Open the app. It starts on an empty scene, on the Floor Plan tab.
  2. Tap the label at the top left of the plan and give the scene its number and heading.
  3. Tap ＋ at the bottom left and add a wall or two, then a character.
  4. Tap ＋ again and add a camera. Drag it into place and turn it toward the character.
  5. Open the Shot List tab. The camera is already there as a shot. Give it a size, a type and a line of description.
  6. Menu › Export › Export PDF… makes the floor plan and the shot list as a PDF.

To look around a finished scene first, tap Open the Sample Scene on an empty plan. Sample Scene… is also in the Library, above Templates, and under Menu › Import. It is an invented scene in a folder of its own, and Reset the Sample puts it back as it was.

That is the whole idea: a camera on the plan is already a shot on the list. The rest of this guide is each of those steps in full.

The screen

  1. 1Menu. Scenes, importing, exporting, sync and backups.
  2. 2Folder and scene. Shows where you are. Tap it for the scenes in this folder, and tap one to go to it.   3. 3The tabs. Script, then the scene’s four views: Breakdown, Strategy, Floor Plan, Shot List. They run in the order the work goes.

  8. 8Undo and Redo, then Select, Draw, Frames, Fit to Screen and Rotate the Floor Plan.
  9. 9Playback. Turn on the animation view, play the blocking, and step through its Times.

The card at the top left of the plan is the scene’s heading. Tap it to add scene info.

Scenes and folders

Scenes live in folders. A folder is usually one episode or one film, and importing a script makes a folder for it.

Where: tap the folder and scene name in the top bar, then Show Scene Library; or Menu › Open Scene…. On the Breakdown, Strategy and Shot List tabs the Library sits down the left side.
  • The folder and scene name in the top bar lists the scenes of the folder you are in. Tap one to go straight to it.
  • New scene and New folder are at the top of the Library.
  • Drag a scene by its grip to move it into a folder. The ••• beside a scene has the rest.
  • Scenes outside any folder are listed under Not in a folder.
  • The Library also holds Templates and the Trash.

Search

Finds a scene by anything written in it, and takes you to the exact passage.

Where: press ⌘F on a computer, or Menu › Open Scene… and type in the search box at the top.
  • It looks through scene numbers, titles, headings and Scene info, folders and characters, shot descriptions and shot notes, the Breakdown, the Strategy and its cuts, checklists, scout notes and the notes in Characters through the story.
  • Each passage that matches is listed under its scene, with a few words around it and the words you typed marked. Tap it and the app opens that scene on the right tab, with the passage in view and the words marked there too.
  • Type words in any order. Capitals, accents and curly apostrophes do not matter: dont reveal finds “Don’t reveal”. Put words in quotation marks to find them together, as in "keep the doorway empty".
  • Scenes in the Trash are not searched. Search reads what is on this device, so writing done on another device shows up once it has synced.

The four tabs

Each tab has its own chapter. Prep, Scout and Set are covered in chapters 9 and 10.

Before them is the Script tab: the script’s own pages, to read and to mark up. It comes first because the work starts there. See The script’s pages.

Saving, Undo and earlier versions

There is no Save button. Work is saved as you go, and the status at the top right says so.

  • Undo and Redo are on the toolbar.
  • Earlier Versions… keeps a copy every five minutes while you work, covering about the last six hours, plus one a day for 30 days. Restoring one replaces what is there now, and you can undo that.
  • Save a Version Now… keeps a copy under a name you choose until you delete it.
  • Back Up Everything makes a file you can keep anywhere. Restore from Backup… reads one back in. The file holds your scenes, your writing and the pictures they still use. If pictures your work uses have not reached this device yet, the backup tells you; let sync finish and back up again.
  • A different account on the same device. If you sign in to an account other than the one this device’s work was synced with, the app asks first: Add Them to This Account, or Sign Out. Nothing is sent until you choose.
Where: Menu › Sync & Backup.

What it will not do: earlier versions live on the device you are using. They do not travel to your other devices. For a copy that does, use Back Up Everything.

Signing in and sync

Sync keeps the same scenes on every device. Without it, everything still works and stays on the one device.

Where: tap the status at the top right.
  1. On your other device, Sign in with the same email and password.
  • Work done offline is sent when you are back online.
  • The status says what sync is doing: Uploading 3 changes… while your work goes up, Downloading pictures 3 of 12… while pictures arrive, and Synced when both are done. Pictures follow the scenes, one at a time.
  • The Sync window shows when the cloud last confirmed your work, with Sync now and Sign out.
  • If the status says Sync paused, tap it. The window says why. Your work is saved on the device meanwhile, and the app keeps trying by itself.
  • Sync is invitation only for now. To request access, write to info@thedirectorsnotebook.com and give the email you sign in with. Until you are added, everything still works and stays on the one device.
  • Once you are added, there is no new account to make. Sign in with that same email and password on each device.

Your synced work is locked

When your account syncs, the app locks your work with your password, on your device, before it is sent. The copy in the cloud can be opened only with your password or your recovery code. The person who runs the app has neither.

You closed the lock window without lockingThe status says Lock to sync. Your work stays on the device and nothing is sent until it is locked. Tap the status, then Lock and Sync….
An item in the cloud will not open on this deviceThe status says Synced · 1 unreadable. It was sealed with a key this device does not have. Open the app on your other devices and let them sync. Work made on this device is not affected.
  • Keep the recovery code. If you forget your password and do not have the code, no one can open the copy of your work in the cloud. Save as a File, Email It to Myself and Print are on the window. Keep it somewhere that is not the device. The window closes only when you tap I Saved It. If the app was closed before you did, the Sync window tells you and offers the code again, or a new one.
  • New Recovery Code… and Change Password… are in the Sync window: tap the status at the top right.
  • If you forget your password: reset it by email, sign in, then tap Use Recovery Code….
  • With neither the password nor the code, a device that still holds your work can start over: Use Recovery Code…, then Start Fresh…. It erases the locked copy in the cloud and sends that device’s work up again.

What it will not do: the lock covers the copy in the cloud. Work on your own devices, your Back Up Everything files and your PDFs are not locked.

Import a Script

Makes a folder with one scene for every scene in the script, and a character for everyone who speaks.

Where: Menu › Import › Import a Script…. Inside a folder’s window it is Import script….
  1. Choose the script: a PDF, a Final Draft file (.fdx), or a Fountain or plain text file. From a PDF, the pages themselves are kept with the new folder, to read and mark up under Script.
  2. Name the new folder, and choose whether it sits at the top level or inside another folder.
  3. Check the characters. Clear the box for anything that is not a character, and set each one’s sex. guess marks the ones the app worked out from the script, so look at those.
  4. Leave the two boxes checked to have saved set templates added at matching locations, and each scene’s characters placed in the scene ready to block.
  5. Tap Import.

Each scene arrives with its number, heading and script pages. One Undo takes the whole import back. If the device holds only the empty scene it started with, that scene is taken away.

A revised draft: in the folder’s window, Import script… again. The app compares the new draft with the scenes you have, marks each one changed, new or omitted in the color of the draft, updates headings, numbers and page counts, and leaves your blocking and writing where they are. One Undo takes it back.

What it will not do: it reads the text inside a PDF. A scanned script, which is only pictures of pages, has no text to read. Dialogue set in two columns side by side in a PDF may come in out of order.

Scene info

The facts about a scene that appear on its pages and printouts.

Where: tap the scene’s card at the top left of the plan, or Menu › This Scene › Scene Info…, or ⌘I. The Breakdown and Strategy tabs have a Scene info button too.
  • Scene number, title and scene heading.
  • Episode or code, shoot day and shoot date.
  • Script pages, in eighths.
  • Act or sequence that starts at this scene. Type a name here, such as ACT TWO, and the Spine shows the act beginning at this scene.
  • Synopsis.

Breakdown

The Breakdown tab asks one question: what does the scene mean?

  • The columns are Synopsis, Snapshot, Imagery & Tone, The Core, Elevating the Scene and Pitfalls. Each starts with prompts, such as Scene Needs and Actor Direction Tips under The Core.
  • + Label adds a prompt of your own to a column.
  • Columns… changes the columns themselves.
  • Story marks the scene as A, B, C or D story. Edit… changes that list.
  • Questions column adds a column for open questions. They are gathered for the whole folder in the Overview.
  • The bar above the columns formats the words you select: a font, a size, bold, italic, underline, strikethrough and a text color. Tx clears those. Align, the two list buttons and the two indent buttons act on the paragraph the cursor is in, or on every paragraph you have selected. The Strategy tab has the same bar.

Strategy

The Strategy tab asks the next question: how do we make the meaning visible?

  • The sections are Power Dynamics (start, end and main event), Camera Strategy, Key Visuals, Blocking Rationale, Arc of Scene and Geometry, and Story and Tone Meeting Notes.
  • + Add a section adds your own. The ⋯ on a section has its options.
  • + Add a cut to another scene records a cut you are planning between this scene and another.

The script’s pages

The script’s own pages, inside the notebook: to read beside your shots, and to mark up with the Apple Pencil.

Where: the Script tab. In Set mode: Script, beside Floor Plan, at the top of the screen.
  1. Import the script from its PDF, and its pages are kept with the new folder. For a folder you already have, open Script and tap Add the Script’s PDF….
  2. Choose a tool from the row of symbols above the page, and mark the page with the Pencil. Hold a finger on a symbol, or rest the mouse on it, to see its name.
  3. One finger moves the page, and a flick keeps it going. Two fingers zoom. Two taps on a blank part of the page fit it to the width. On a Mac, scroll, and draw with the mouse.

Import a Breakdown and Strategy

Brings in breakdowns and strategies you have already written in Word.

Where: Menu › Import › Import a Breakdown and Strategy…. On the Breakdown tab it is Import from Word….
  • It reads a Word file (.docx) and looks for Director’s Notebook and Shooting Strategy pages.
  • Pages are matched to scenes in the folder by scene number. A scene that does not exist yet is made.
  • A scene that already has writing in it is left alone unless you choose to replace it.

What it will not do: it cannot open the older .doc format. Save the file as .docx first.

Characters through the story

For one character, every scene they are in, in story order, with six short notes of your own for each scene. Useful when you shoot out of order and need the character’s place in the story on the day.

Where: Characters… on the Breakdown tab; a character’s menu on the plan › Through the Story…. In Set mode the scene’s characters appear in a Characters fold.
NoteFor
Coming inWhere the character is as the scene starts. Until you write one, the box shows what you wrote for Leaving in their previous scene.
What changesWhat turns for them in the scene.
LeavingWhere they are when it ends.
What they knowWhat they know at this point, and what they do not know yet: “She has heard the accusation, but she has not seen the evidence.”
What they wantWhat they are after in the scene. Also on the actor’s page.
What’s in the wayWhat stands between them and it. Also on the actor’s page.
  • Everything is optional. Empty boxes take no room anywhere else.
  • A character is in a scene when they are on its floor plan or in its cast. The buttons at the top switch to another character; Open goes to that scene; Actor’s Page… opens the character’s page for that scene.
  • The notes are saved with each scene, so they sync, back up and come back with an earlier version like the Breakdown. If two devices change the same note, both versions are kept, marked OTHER VERSION.
  • Copy as Text puts the whole arc on the clipboard, ready to paste into an email or a document.
  • Search finds these notes too.

Checklist

A to-do list that belongs to one scene. Add items, check them off, and clear the finished ones with Remove Finished.

Where: Menu › This Scene › Checklist…, or the Checklist button on the Breakdown and Strategy tabs.

The folder’s Overview

Shows the whole episode or film at once, in seven views.

Where: Menu › the line that names your folder, ending in Overview & Questions….
ViewWhat it shows
ScenesEach scene with its progress on the Breakdown, Strategy, Floor Plan and Shot List, and how many questions are open.
SpineThe scenes as blocks in story order, sized by page count, setups or time estimate, with a band for day or night. Tap a scene to see what it is, or to start an act or a sequence there. Below it, where each story lives.
CutsThe last frame of each scene beside the first frame of the next, so you can judge every cut between scenes. Above them, How scenes meet, by size: each scene’s opening and closing size on one line, wide, medium and close each in its own color. A scene opens on the first frame of its animatic and ends on its last, unless you choose Opens on or Ends on. A size taken only from the Shot List’s order is dashed.
Visual LanguageThe episode’s visual ideas, each with the shots that carry it, in story order. See Visual Language.
DaysEach shoot day with its scenes, pages, setups and shots, and the estimated time of its shots against the hours the day has. It tells you how much is to spare, or how far over. Call … · Wrap … sets a day’s call, wrap and lunch. Export This Day… makes one PDF of the day’s scenes: see Export this day. Owed shots given this day to pick up on are counted here, under Pickups.
OwedEvery shot marked Owed, scene by scene, each with the day to pick it up on. See Shots owed.
QuestionsEverything written in the scenes’ Questions columns.
  • A scene belongs to the day typed under Shoot day in Scene info. Times come from the Est. time column of the shot list.
  • Cameras in one setup roll together, so their time is counted once, at the longest. Dropped shots are not counted.
  • Until you set them, a day runs from a 7:00 AM call to a 7:00 PM wrap, with an hour for lunch.

Export PDF… in the Overview prints the view you are on. In the Days view, each day has its own Export This Day….

Visual Language

A visual idea is a motif you repeat and change, or a rule for the camera: thresholds and doorways, reflections, the camera waits for Maya. Link the shots that carry it, and the Overview shows them side by side in story order.

Where: the folder’s Overview › Visual Language. From a shot: tap its camera on the floor plan › Visual Language….
  1. Tap New Visual Idea…. Name it, choose Visual motif or Camera rule, and, if you like, write what it does for the story.
  2. Tap Link a Shot… and choose the shot. If you like, say what this use is (Introduced, Repeated, Transformed, Deliberately broken, or a word of your own) and why it belongs here.
  3. Tap a card to change its link, open its shot, or remove it.
  • Ideas belong to the folder whose Overview you are in: one episode, or one film.
  • Cards run in story order, whatever order you linked them in. Contact Sheet shows the frames larger. A shot with no frame yet can still be linked.
  • A shot with more than one version asks whether the link is for the whole shot or one version.
  • A link names the shot itself, not its number, so renumbering never breaks it. If the shot’s scene goes to Trash, the link waits, and comes back with the scene.
  • Export PDF… prints each idea with its intention and its shots, two to a row: something to share with your director of photography and production designer.
  • Search finds what you wrote in a link, and opens its idea.

Doors and windows

Positions and moves

Times and playback

A Time is a beat of the scene. Everything set to play in the same Time moves together.

Where: the sun button on the toolbar, with Play and the Times beside it.

  • Tap a Time’s number, then tap a position to put it in that Time. Tap it again to take it out.

  • Play, or Space, plays the blocking.
  • The eye button shows the other Times faded, or hides them.
  • Tap the sun again, or press Esc, to go back to editing.

A Time’s menu: speed, labels and order

Where: in the animation view, tap the Time you are on.

  • Insert Section Label… names a stretch of the scene on the toolbar.

  • Slow, Normal and Fast set how fast playback runs. Loop plays it again and again.

Pages and blocking plans

Where: the Pages button on the toolbar.

Selecting several things

  • Select on the toolbar, or G, draws a lasso around pieces.

Clear Moves

Starts the blocking over without rebuilding the set or placing anyone again.

  • With something selected, you can clear only that.

The actor’s page

One character’s path through one scene, to hold in a blocking rehearsal without showing anyone the shot list. The character’s marks are numbered in the order they hit them, with the moves between.   • Reach to a Subject or a Spot… stretches the wedge to a character and keeps it on them.

Track With

For a camera that travels with a walk: leading, beside or trailing.

Where: the camera’s menu › Track With… › tap the character.
  • The camera gets a position for each of the character’s, in the same Time, so they move together.
  • Choose the side, how far off in feet, and whether it rides Leading, Beside or Trailing.
  • Lay dolly track along it puts track under the move in 4 ft sections.
  • Change the walk and the camera follows.

What it will not do: Leading rides ahead and to the side, not straight in front. And once you drag the camera yourself it stops traveling with the character and keeps its positions. Use Track With… again to relink it.

Storyboard frames

  • Add Storyboard Frame… on a camera attaches a picture to that shot.
  • Frames on the toolbar, or B, shows or hides them on the plan.
  • The folder’s Overview uses each scene’s first and last frames in its Cuts view, or the first and last of its animatic, when it has one.

The animatic

Watch the scene before you shoot it. The animatic plays a scene’s storyboard frames in the order they will cut together, each held for the time you give it, silent. It gives the scene a running time to set beside its page count.

Where: Shot List ▾ › Animatic….
  1. The first time, tap Start with Every Frame in Shot List Order, or Pick Frames… to choose them yourself.
  2. Each frame holds for two seconds. Under each frame, choose a longer or shorter time.
  3. Drag a frame along the strip to move it. Or tap it, then Move Earlier, Move Later, Use Again (when the cut comes back to a shot) or Remove.
  4. ▶ Play plays it. Export Video… records it as a silent video. It records as it plays, so it takes as long as the scene runs.
  • Add Frames… shows every frame of the scene, shot by shot. Each tap adds one where the cut is open; the window stays open until Done.
  • Start Over… puts back every frame in Shot List order, two seconds each. Undo brings your cut back.
  • The running time is only as good as the holds. A dialogue scene runs as long as its lines take; there, the page count is the better guide.
  • Each scene has its own animatic. It syncs with the scene.

Sun & Shadows

For exteriors. It shows where the sun is at a place, date and time, the shadows it casts, and how each camera is lit.

Where: Layers › Sun & Shadows. It is set for one scene at a time.
  1. Choose the Place. Type a city, tap Use Where I Am, or enter Coordinates…
  2. Check the Date. It comes from the scene’s shoot date in Scene Info, or you can set it here.
  3. Turn north with the slider until the N arrow on the plan points the right way.
  4. Drag Time to move the sun.

The panel then tells you:

  • Where the sun is coming from, how high it is, and how long the shadows are.
  • Sunrise, high point, sunset and the golden hours.
  • How each camera is lit at that time: Front-lit, Side-lit, Looking into the sun, High sun or Sun is down. It also gives the hours when that camera would be front-lit.

Smaller shrinks the panel to a bar with the time on it. Hide Sun turns it off.

What it will not do: Shadows are drawn from typical heights, such as 9 ft for a wall, and only for what is on the plan. A building or a tree you have not drawn casts no shadow. The place must be on the city list, your own position, or coordinates.

Night Look

For night scenes. The plan goes dark and stays lit only where its lights throw.

Where: Layers › Night Look. It is set for one scene at a time.

What lights the dark:

  • Film lights from Add Lighting, along their beams.
  • A pool around a Practical Lamp, Floor Lamp, Street Lamp, Candle, Campfire, Fire or Fireplace.
  • A Flashlight.
  • Vehicles, once you switch on Headlights On in the vehicle’s menu. The switch appears while Night Look is on.

Darkness sets how dark the rest of the plan is.

  • Turning on Night Look turns Sun & Shadows off, and the other way around.
  • The PDF export has a Night Look option, for scenes that have it turned on.

Lighting pieces

Where: Add › Add Lighting…, or press L.
  • The pieces are Fresnel, LED Panel, Softbox, Kino Flo, Practical Lamp and Sun / Window Light.
  • The film lights draw a beam on the plan. Turn the light to aim it.
  • The light’s menu › Details… has a Beam slider for how far it reaches. All the way down hides the beam.
  • Lighting has its own row in Layers, so you can fade it or lock it.

Shots owed

A shot you didn’t finish on its day is owed. Mark it Owed, give it a day to pick it up on, and the notebook carries it to that day.

  1. In Set mode, tap Owed on the shot. Or set its Status in the Shot List.
  2. Under the buttons, choose Pick up on… and the day.
  • The folder’s Overview has an Owed view: every owed shot, scene by scene, each with its pickup day, which you can choose right there. Export PDF… prints the list for your first assistant director.
  • With a pickup day, the shot’s time moves from its scene’s day to the pickup day. In Days it is listed under that day’s Pickups, and Export This Day… lists the pickups on the day’s first page and adds their scenes after the day’s own.
  • Without a pickup day, it stays on its scene’s day, still to shoot.
  • Marked Shot on its pickup day, it leaves the Owed list and stays counted on that day. Set back to Planned or Ready, or Dropped, it goes home to its scene’s day.
  • The days offered are the shoot days in the episode’s Scene info.

The blocking as a video

Where: Menu › Export › Save the Blocking as a Video…, or Save as Video… in a Time’s menu.

Who can sync

  • Sync is invitation only for now. Make your free account first (Chapter 1). Then write to info@thedirectorsnotebook.com and give the email you sign in with.
  • Once you are added, there is no new account to make. On the device you already use, sync starts by itself. On each other device, tap Sign in with that same email and password.
  • Sync needs a confirmed email. If yours isn’t confirmed yet, tap the status at the top right, then Send the Link. The Sync window offers it even while sync is working. Open the link in the email, come back and tap I’ve Confirmed. Sync connects by itself.
  • Until then, everything still works. Your work stays on your own device.

What it will not do: Sync does not show anyone your scenes, or show you theirs. Each account syncs its own work to its own devices. To hand someone your plans, send them the PDFs.

What syncs

Sync is invitation only for now. Once your account has been added and you sign in, these travel to every device you sign in on:

  • Scenes, with their sets, blocking, cameras and shots.
  • The writing on the Breakdown and Strategy tabs.
  • Images: storyboard frames, plans and scout photos.
  • Folders, locations and scouts, with each folder’s visual ideas.
  • A folder’s script: its PDF, and the marks you make on its pages.
  • My Props, and your choice of shot list columns.

These stay on the device where you made them:

  • Earlier Versions, and the copies made with Save a Version Now.
  • Anything too large to sync, which the Ready for Set check names. A very long sketch is the usual cause.

When two devices have both been working, new material from both is kept. When the same scene was changed on both, each part changed on only one device is kept from that device: the title, heading, Scene info, story, checklist, cast, sun and night. Where both changed the same part, the newer change wins, and the older version of the scene is kept as a Blocking Plan (Pages menu). Both devices say so. Writing in the Breakdown and Strategy is merged box by box; a box written on both keeps both, marked OTHER VERSION.
